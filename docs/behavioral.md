# Behavioral interview practice

Use real examples from Jampak and your past engineering work. Do not invent impact or metrics. Replace every bracket with evidence you can defend.

## STAR worksheet

- **Situation:** Context, customer/system, constraints, and stakes in 2–3 sentences.
- **Task:** Your specific ownership and definition of success.
- **Action:** Decisions you personally made, alternatives, collaboration, debugging evidence, and tradeoffs. Use “I” accurately.
- **Result:** Customer/business/engineering outcome with real `[metric]`, what you learned, and what you would change.

## Prompts

1. Tell me about a feature you owned end-to-end.
2. Tell me about a difficult production bug.
3. Tell me about a technical tradeoff.
4. Tell me about receiving code review feedback.
5. Tell me about disagreeing with a technical approach.
6. Tell me about helping a junior developer.
7. Tell me about operating a production service.
8. Tell me about balancing speed and code quality.
9. Tell me about a time requirements were ambiguous.
10. Tell me about a system you improved.

For each, ask yourself: What was uniquely mine? What evidence changed my mind? Who disagreed? What did users experience? What was the before/after `[real number]`? What follow-up made the improvement durable?

## Jampak story inventory

Create short titles only at first: `[feature shipped]`, `[incident diagnosed]`, `[architecture decision]`, `[cross-team ambiguity]`, `[quality/reliability improvement]`, `[mentoring moment]`. Reuse one story for different competencies only by changing the emphasis honestly. Aim for 2–3 minutes, then prepare deeper technical follow-ups.
